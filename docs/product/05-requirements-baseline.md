## 1. Product and delivery

The new direction serves an ordinary individual consumer. The core job is personal daily time management. Public sharing and team collaboration are auxiliary. Personal time management has priority over team management. (DEC-012)

A Calendar is a container. An Event is an entry in a Calendar. One Calendar contains multiple Events. One Event is in exactly one Calendar at a time. Public or private visibility, recurrence, conflict warnings, and occurrence or series changes apply to the Event. The personal calendar page and a team Calendar are Calendars. A sharing link is an address for one existing Calendar, not another Calendar. The word Schedule is not the formal name of either object. (DEC-014, DEC-040)

| Delivery | What the user can do | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| First | Register, log in, keep one personal Calendar, and create, change, and delete timed Events, including public or private visibility, daily, weekly, and monthly repetition, and conflict warnings. Change the calendar timezone. Delete the account. Use a browser. The server holds the business data. | DEC-013, DEC-016, DEC-018, DEC-022 |
| After the first and before desktop | Search by username or nickname, open a personal calendar page, use a sharing link, and use the confirmed team rules. | DEC-013, DEC-015, DEC-021, DEC-040 |
| Last | Use a desktop app connected to the same server, with the same identity, rules, and data. | DEC-013 |

The first delivery is the MVP boundary in this draft. Later deliveries are confirmed product rules with their own boundary. They are not first-delivery behavior. No web framework, server framework, or desktop shell is selected.

## 2. Actors

| Actor | Who | First delivery | Later confirmed delivery |
|---|---|---|---|
| User | A person with one verified email and password, one unchanging username, and an optional nickname | Registers, logs in, uses one personal Calendar, and may delete the account | May also search, open another user's public Calendar, share a link, create teams, and join teams |
| Creator | The user who created an Event | Sees and edits that Event, including a private Event | Same ownership after the Event moves between Calendars |
| Other logged-in user | A different user who has logged in | Cannot open another person's Calendar and cannot see that person's Events | Can see a public Event's full content and cannot see a private Event |
| Logged-out person | A person who is not logged in, including the owner after logout | Cannot open a personal Calendar and cannot see any Event on it | Continuing still requires login before another person's public Calendar can be opened |

## 3. First-delivery flows

### 3.1 Register and log in

The person submits a username, an email address, and a password. The nickname may be empty. A nickname that is empty after outer whitespace is removed counts as no nickname, and the display name is the username. Outer whitespace is removed before a non-empty nickname is stored. Display and nickname search use that stored value, and spaces inside it remain. (DEC-037, DEC-062) Changing the nickname updates the account display name immediately. A public Event shows the current nickname, or the username when there is none, without another Event save. (DEC-037) The registration cannot log in until the email is verified. Registration sends a one-time proof only to that registration's email. Submitting a matching proof makes the registration a verified account. A proof that does not match, is no longer valid, or is used again fails without revealing whether a registration exists and does not verify it. How long that proof remains valid stays OPEN in DEC-023. (DEC-077) Until the registrant cancels it or the email is verified, that username and email cannot be used for another account. The registrant may cancel the unverified registration without logging in by submitting that registration's email and password. An unknown email, a wrong password, and an already verified account all produce the same failure result, reveal nothing about whether an account exists, and change nothing. Only an unverified registration whose email and password both match is removed, and its username and email may then be registered again. If verification finishes first, the later cancellation fails and the account stays verified. If cancellation finishes first, verification finds no registration and creates no account. A verified account cannot be cancelled by this path. (DEC-049) Only a verified account can complete recovery. Completion sets a new password, and the previous password fails immediately. Every session already open for that account ends and cannot continue editing. A save that has been submitted but has not finished writes nothing, and the state before recovery completed remains. A save that finished before recovery completed is kept. (DEC-061, DEC-073) Completion itself does not open the personal Calendar. A later login with the new password opens it. (DEC-061) An unknown email and an unverified registration produce the same non-revealing failure result as a failed login. Recovery does not verify a registration. An email that already belongs to an unverified or verified account cannot register a second account. A taken username, an email held by an unverified registration, and an email held by a verified account all produce the same registration failure. That result does not say which value is taken or whether an account is verified. Starting recovery shows the same result for an unknown email, an unverified registration, and a verified account, and does not reveal whether an account exists or is verified. Starting recovery sends the one-time proof only to a verified account. An unknown email and an unverified registration send nothing. (DEC-051, DEC-075) Only a verified account can later complete recovery. Completion submits the new password and a one-time proof sent only to that verified account's email. An unknown email, an unverified registration, a proof that does not match, a proof that is no longer valid, and a second use of the same proof all produce the same non-revealing failure and change nothing. Sending a later proof makes every unused earlier proof no longer valid. The newest proof can still complete recovery. How long the proof remains valid stays OPEN in DEC-023. (DEC-051, DEC-066, DEC-076) A login with a verified email and the correct password opens that user's personal Calendar. An unknown email, an unverified registration, and a wrong password all produce the same failure result and do not reveal whether an account exists. (DEC-015, DEC-024, DEC-025)

### 3.2 Keep a personal Calendar

The verified user has a personal Calendar. The user sees both public and private Events on it. No other user can open it in the first delivery. A logged-out person, including the owner after logout, cannot open the personal Calendar and cannot see any Event on it. Continuing requires login. After login, the owner sees that owner's Calendar. (DEC-014, DEC-016, DEC-038)

### 3.3 Save an Event

A new Event is private unless the creator chooses public before saving. A save requires a title, a start, and an end after the start. After outer whitespace is removed, the title must still contain a character. Location and description may be empty. There is no date-only Event. A save that lacks a required value does not write, and a stored Event remains unchanged. A successful edit of a non-repeating Event replaces its stored content immediately and does not keep the previous content. A successful deletion removes that Event immediately, does not restore it, and excludes it from later conflict comparison. A failed or cancelled deletion leaves the stored Event unchanged. (DEC-034) The same account may save twice. Each successful save replaces the stored Event with that save's complete content. When both succeed, the stored Event is the content of the save that finishes last. A failed save writes nothing. A save that finishes after account deletion has completed writes nothing. If the save finishes first and account deletion then completes, deletion still removes the login, personal Calendar, and every Event, including that save. An operation that does not finish leaves the prior state. (DEC-039, DEC-050) An Event that has already ended, an Event that crosses the current instant, and a series that starts in the past may be saved. (DEC-017, DEC-019)

### 3.4 Change one occurrence or the series

Deleting one occurrence removes only that occurrence and keeps it absent. The absence remembers that occurrence's original start instant. A later clock-time change on the same weekday does not create the new clock time on that date. Other occurrences on that weekday that have not started use the new time. A later clock-time change also leaves a deleted daily date and a deleted monthly date empty. Other dates that have not started use the new time. (DEC-052, DEC-067, DEC-074) A weekday change does not move the absence onto the new weekday, and the new weekday's other occurrences appear. Timezone re-expansion does not recreate that occurrence at the new clock instant. (DEC-052) A later series save may change occurrences that have not started, but it does not restore a deleted occurrence and does not reattach a detached occurrence. If the series save finishes first and the deletion or detachment finishes later, the later change wins. A detachment that finishes later stores the complete content submitted with that detachment. Changes the series save already made are not on that occurrence. (DEC-050, DEC-068) Deleting the series removes those absence records with the series. The absence is not shown as a cancelled Event. (DEC-031) Modifying one occurrence changes only that occurrence and detaches it from the series. Changing or deleting the series does not affect a detached occurrence. Deleting the series removes occurrences that are still attached and keeps detached occurrences. A series time change applies only to attached occurrences that have not started. An attached occurrence has occurred when its start has been reached, including while it is still in progress. A series time change and a timezone re-expansion do not move it. This applies to daily, weekly, and monthly series. (DEC-029) (DEC-019)

The creator may later make one occurrence or the whole series public or private. Changing one occurrence's visibility detaches it. A series visibility change updates every occurrence still attached, including occurrences that have started, and does not change a detached occurrence. For an occurrence that has started, a series save that changes visibility and does not change title, location, or description updates visibility only and leaves its time and other content unchanged. A series change to title, location, or description updates every still-attached occurrence, including one that has started, and that started occurrence keeps its time. A detached occurrence keeps its own text, time, and visibility. In one series save, each field follows its own rule. A started occurrence that is still attached receives the new visibility, title, location, and description, and keeps its time. An occurrence that has not started receives the new time, visibility, title, location, and description. (DEC-017, DEC-032, DEC-033, DEC-054)

### 3.5 Repeat

Daily occurs on every calendar day in the user's current calendar timezone, at the start clock time, for the start-to-end duration. Weekly occurs only on the weekday of its start. Monthly occurs on the same date each month, and that date must exist in every month. None of the three ends automatically. The choice among non-repeating, daily, weekly, and monthly is fixed at creation. A later edit cannot change it. An edit that attempts the change writes nothing, and the stored Event remains unchanged. The creator may delete the Event and create another with a different kind. (DEC-035) If a monthly date does not exist in every month, the preview names the types of month that lack the date and lists the concrete missing dates only inside the same 90 calendar dates used for conflict comparison, starting on the save date and including that date. It does not list every such month for the life of the series. The series is not saved. The product does not move the date to the month's last day and does not skip the month. (DEC-018, DEC-019, DEC-053, DEC-065)

### 3.6 Conflict warning

Saving compares the Event with every other Event that creator still owns. In the first delivery that set is the personal Calendar. The 90 days are 90 calendar dates in the creator's current calendar timezone, starting on the calendar date of the save and including that date. Other repeating Events are expanded across those dates. The occurrence or series being saved is not compared with its own attached occurrences. A detached occurrence is another Event the creator still owns. Saving the series compares it with that occurrence and warns when they overlap. (DEC-058) One saved occurrence is compared only with other Events. Intervals overlap only when they share a positive length. An end that equals the next start is not an overlap. An overlap is warned when the shared time falls inside the window, including an overlap entirely before the save instant and an overlap with an occurrence that has already started. An occurrence that crosses the far boundary counts when any positive shared length is inside the window. A save with no overlap inside the window lists no overlapping Event and still succeeds. A series save still says the repetition continues after 90 days. That sentence is not a conflict warning. When overlaps exist inside the window, the warning lists each overlapping Event's title, start, and end, does not show an email address, and does not block saving. Saving a series also says the repetition continues after 90 days. That sentence means only that the series continues. It does not mean a conflict was found after 90 days. An overlap entirely after the window is not detected and is not listed. (DEC-020, DEC-030, DEC-036, DEC-053)

### 3.7 Timezone

Registration sets the calendar timezone from the browser. The user may change it. Entry, recurrence, the 90-day window, and displayed clock times use the current timezone. When the timezone changes and the series is not edited, an attached occurrence that has already occurred keeps its absolute instant and changes only its displayed clock time. An attached occurrence that has not yet occurred is expanded again at the same clock time in the new timezone. A future occurrence whose doubled start the creator has already chosen keeps that chosen absolute instant, and the new timezone changes only its displayed clock time. (DEC-027, DEC-070) A detached or individually deleted occurrence is unchanged. A local time that does not exist cannot be saved when the user is entering it. A local time that occurs twice must be chosen by the user when the user is entering it. A generated occurrence keeps its clock-face duration. If its generated start does not exist on that date, no occurrence is created, the series remains, and the creator can see that the date was skipped. If its generated start occurs twice, no occurrence is created until the creator chooses one instant in the timezone that applies at the choice. The creator can see that a choice is required. Before that choice, it is not included in conflict comparison. When the generated start exists and the generated end does not, or the interval would cross a missing local hour, that occurrence is skipped, the series remains, and the creator can see that the date was skipped. It is not shortened and is not stored with a nonexistent clock time. After a later timezone change, a skipped date stays skipped, and an unchosen doubled start stays unchosen. A later series time change also leaves that skipped date empty, even when the new clock time exists on that date. Other dates that have not started use the new time. The creator is still offered that start's two original absolute instants, labeled in the timezone that applies at the choice, even when the current timezone no longer doubles the clock time. (DEC-018, DEC-027, DEC-028, DEC-057, DEC-069, DEC-071)

### 3.8 Delete the account

The user may delete the account. The login, the personal Calendar, and every Event on it are deleted immediately and are not restored. Deletion has one result: those three disappear together, or the account stays unchanged. A deletion that stops early does not remove the login while leaving the Events, and does not remove the Events while leaving the login. (DEC-039) The username is not assigned again. The email may register a new account with a new username. The new account does not own the deleted Events. (DEC-022)

| AC-P28 | BR-P5, DEC-032 | Given one occurrence of a private series is made public, when the creator later makes the series private and changes its time, then the detached occurrence stays public at its previous time and the not-yet-started attached occurrences become private at the new time. An attached occurrence that has started becomes private and keeps its time. |
| AC-P29 | DEC-033, DEC-054 | Given a series has a started occurrence and a not-yet-started occurrence, when the creator changes the series title, location, description, and visibility in one save, then the started occurrence shows the new text and visibility and keeps its time. The not-yet-started occurrence shows the new text, visibility, and time. A detached occurrence is unchanged. |
| AC-P19 | BR-P8 | Given a daily Event, when the series repeats, then an attached occurrence exists on each later calendar day at the start clock time in the user's current calendar timezone. |
| AC-P20 | BR-P8 | Given a monthly date that exists in every month, when the user saves the series, then the series is stored. |
| AC-P31 | BR-P8, DEC-035 | Given a saved daily Event, when the creator edits it and selects monthly, then nothing is written and the stored Event remains daily. |
| AC-P21 | BR-P9, DEC-031, DEC-050, DEC-052, DEC-067, DEC-074 | Given a weekly series, when the creator deletes one Wednesday occurrence and later changes the series to Thursday, then that original start stays absent and the Thursday occurrences appear. A timezone re-expansion does not recreate the deleted occurrence at the new clock instant. A series save that finishes after the deletion does not restore it. If the series save finishes first and the deletion finishes later, the occurrence stays absent. When the deleted occurrence is Wednesday 09:00 and the series later changes to Wednesday 15:00 before that date starts, that date stays empty and other Wednesdays that have not started use 15:00. When a daily 09:00 on 12 May or a monthly 12th at 09:00 is deleted and the series later changes to 15:00 before that date starts, that date stays empty and other dates that have not started use 15:00. |
| AC-P22 | BR-P9 | Given one detached occurrence and other attached occurrences, when the creator deletes the series, then the attached occurrences are gone and the detached occurrence remains. |
| AC-P23 | BR-P11 | Given a local time that occurs twice, when the user chooses one occurrence and saves, then the Event stores that chosen instant. |

## 6. Confirmed rules outside the first delivery

These rules are confirmed. They are not first-delivery acceptance criteria.

- Username search returns one user. Nickname search may return several users. A personal calendar link uses the username. (DEC-015)
- After an account is deleted, username search finds no user. Nickname search does not list that deleted account. Opening its old personal-calendar link opens no Calendar and shows no Event, whether or not the visitor is logged in. A later account that reuses the email has its own username and its own link. The old username and the old link do not open that new account. (DEC-047)
- Each search result shows the current nickname and the username. With no nickname, the display name is the username and the result still includes the username. The email is not shown. A username search matches the entire username and returns at most that one account, with the same fields. A nickname search lists only accounts whose current stored nickname equals the entire searched text. A search for `Sam` finds a nickname saved from ` Sam `. A search for ` Sam ` does not. It does not list a nickname that merely contains that text. Another person's username does not appear in a username search merely because it equals some nickname. A logged-out person cannot search. A logged-in search with no match returns no user and does not say whether the username never existed or was retired. An empty search returns no user and does not list accounts. (DEC-048, DEC-062, DEC-064)
- A logged-in user who can open a Calendar sees a public Event's title, start, end, location, description, username, and current nickname. With no nickname, that user sees the username. The nickname shown is the current account display name and does not require another Event save. An empty location or description is shown empty. The email is not shown. A private Event is not visible and does not reveal its existence. Another logged-in viewer sees the same absolute start and end converted to that viewer's current calendar timezone, and the timezone is shown. The creator continues to see the creator's timezone. The stored instant does not change. Whether two Events overlap remains an absolute comparison. Opening another person's public Calendar requires login. (DEC-016, DEC-017, DEC-037, DEC-056)
- A user may create more than one team and belong to more than one team. Each team has exactly one team Calendar. The creator invites by username, and the invited person joins only by accepting. A member creates Events on that Calendar and may modify or delete only Events that member created. The creator of an Event that remains after the member leaves may still modify or delete it. The creator cannot edit or see another person's private Event. Leaving or removal ends membership and leaves existing Events in place for their creator to modify or delete. Dissolution while other members remain ends the team Calendar, memberships, and pending invitations, and moves each Event to its creator's personal Calendar without changing visibility. (DEC-021)
- Conflict comparison includes team Events the creator still owns once teams exist. (DEC-020)
- A sharing link is an address for one existing Calendar, not another Calendar. The user creates and revokes links to that user's personal Calendar. The person who created a team creates and revokes links to that team Calendar. One Calendar may have more than one link. Revoking one link removes only that address. The Calendar, its Events, and the other links remain. One Event is in exactly one Calendar at a time. A logged-in user who can open that Calendar sees the same public Events through the link. Private Events stay hidden. A logged-out person sees no Events through a live link. A revoked personal link, a team link revoked while its creator remains a member, a team link that stops because its creator left, a team link after dissolution, and a sharing link after account deletion open no Calendar and show no Event to a logged-out or logged-in person. The result does not say whether the Calendar used to exist. (DEC-040, DEC-059, DEC-072)
- An Event that remains after its creator leaves stays on the team Calendar. That former member opens a list in that member's own account containing only Events that member still owns on teams the member has left. From the list, the former member can see, modify, and delete those Events. The former member cannot open the rest of the team Calendar, cannot see another person's Events, and cannot create a team Event or manage the team. Current members still see the retained Event on the team Calendar according to its visibility. Removal uses this same list. When the former member later accepts an invitation and becomes a current member of that team again, the list entry for that team ends. Those Events remain only on the team Calendar, and the member edits them there. (DEC-041, DEC-055)
- The person who creates a team becomes a member immediately and can create the first team Event. While that person remains a member, that person is the only administrator and handles invitations, removal, dissolution, and creating or revoking the team Calendar's sharing links. When that person leaves, membership and administration both end and nobody succeeds. The team and its Calendar remain. Other members remain and may still create their own Events. Pending invitations end and can no longer be accepted. Existing team sharing links stop opening. The Calendar and its Events are not deleted for that reason. (DEC-042)
- A current member can open the team Calendar, see every public Event, and see private Events that member created. That member cannot see another person's private Event. Any other logged-in user can open the team Calendar and sees only public Events. A logged-out person cannot open it and sees no Event. A former member still cannot open the rest of the team Calendar and uses the retained-Event list from DEC-041. (DEC-043)
- An unknown username creates no invitation and no account. An existing member receives no invitation, and that membership stays unchanged. A pending invitation to the same person for the same team is not duplicated, and the pending invitation remains. Only the invited account can accept, and only while that account is logged in. Another logged-in user cannot accept it and does not become a member. Acceptance after the invitation has ended does not add a member. An invitation ends when it is declined, cancelled by the creator, ended because the creator leaves, or ended because the team is dissolved. A verified invited account sees that invitation in the account after logging in, and only that logged-in account can accept or decline. An unverified username and a retired username create no invitation and no account. The administrator sees current members and pending invitations. Each row shows the current nickname and the username, or the username when there is no nickname, and does not show the email. A current member sees the member list with those fields and does not see pending invitations. (DEC-044, DEC-060)
- The creator may dissolve the team while that creator is the only current member. The result matches dissolution while other members remain. The team Calendar, membership, and pending invitations end. Each Event moves to its original creator's personal Calendar, including an Event left by a former member. An absence moves with its series. Visibility is unchanged. The original creator can still modify or delete Events that creator owns. This does not allow dissolution after the creator has already left. (DEC-045, DEC-052)
- Creating a team and its one team Calendar, accepting an invitation, leaving, removal, and dissolution that moves every Event each finish entirely or leave the prior state unchanged. Dissolution does not leave some Events moved and others still on the team Calendar. An Event is not left in two Calendars, or in none, because a transition stopped halfway. (DEC-063)
- An ended team Event stays on the team Calendar until the team is dissolved. Dissolution moves that ended Event to its original creator's personal Calendar together with the other Events. The rule that an ended occurrence stays on the personal Calendar applies only to an Event stored on that personal Calendar. An ended team Event that its creator still owns continues to participate in that creator's conflict comparison. (DEC-046)
