## 1. Purpose

An ordinary individual consumer manages personal daily time. Public sharing and team collaboration are auxiliary. Personal time management has priority over team management. (DEC-012)

A Calendar is a container. An Event is an entry in a Calendar. One Calendar contains multiple Events. One Event is in exactly one Calendar at a time. Public or private visibility, recurrence, conflict warnings, and occurrence or series changes apply to the Event. The personal calendar page and a team Calendar are Calendars. A sharing link is an address for one existing Calendar, not another Calendar. The word Schedule is not the formal name of either object. (DEC-014, DEC-040)

## 2. Delivery boundary

### First delivery

The first delivery is the MVP. The user reaches it through a browser. The server holds the business data. It includes:

- Register, verify the email, log in, recover the password, and delete the account.
- One personal Calendar for the verified user.
- Create, change, and delete timed Events.
- Public or private visibility.
- Daily, weekly, and monthly repetition.
- Conflict warnings.
- A calendar timezone taken from the browser and changeable by the user.

No web framework, server framework, or desktop shell is selected. (DEC-013, DEC-016, DEC-018, DEC-022)

### After the first delivery and before desktop

These rules are confirmed. They are not first-delivery behavior.

- Search by username or nickname.
- Open a personal calendar page and another person's public Calendar.
- Create and revoke sharing links.
- Create teams, invite by username, and use the confirmed team Calendar rules.

### Last delivery

A desktop app connects to the same server and uses the same identity, rules, and data. It adds no separate product rules. (DEC-013)

### Not in any confirmed delivery

- Username character set and length, password composition, verification-message lifetime, recovery-proof lifetime, and changing the account email. (DEC-023)
- Deleting an account that still belongs to a team. (DEC-022)
- How many personal Calendars one user may own beyond the personal calendar page. (DEC-014)
- A session length. (DEC-038)
- A separate action that stops future repeats while keeping series history. The confirmed exit is to delete the series, or to delete the Event and create another kind. (DEC-035)
- The old desktop product's team, busy/free board, and email roster. Those remain in `docs/product/04-prd.md`.

## 3. Actors

| Actor | Who | First delivery | Later confirmed delivery |
|---|---|---|---|
| User | A person with one verified email and password, one unchanging username, and an optional nickname | Registers, logs in, uses one personal Calendar, and may delete the account | May also search, open another user's public Calendar, share a link, create teams, and join teams |
| Creator | The user who created an Event | Sees and edits that Event, including a private Event | Same ownership after the Event moves between Calendars |
| Other logged-in user | A different user who has logged in | Cannot open another person's Calendar and cannot see that person's Events | Can see another person's public Event in full. Cannot see another person's private Event. A team member sees a private Event that member created |
| Logged-out person | A person who is not logged in, including the owner after logout | Cannot open a personal Calendar and cannot see any Event on it | Still cannot open another person's public Calendar or search |

## 4. First-delivery flows

### 4.1 Register and log in

The person submits a username, an email address, and a password. The nickname may be empty. A nickname that is empty after outer whitespace is removed counts as no nickname, and the display name is the username. Outer whitespace is removed before a non-empty nickname is stored. Display uses that stored value, and spaces inside it remain. Changing the nickname updates the account display name immediately. A public Event shows the current nickname, or the username when there is none, without another Event save. (DEC-037, DEC-062)

The registration cannot log in until the email is verified. Registration sends a one-time proof only to that registration's email. Submitting a matching proof makes the registration a verified account. A proof that does not match, is no longer valid, or is used again fails without revealing whether a registration exists and does not verify it. How long that proof remains valid stays OPEN in DEC-023. (DEC-077) Until the registrant cancels it or the email is verified, that username and email cannot be used for another account. The registrant may cancel the unverified registration without logging in by submitting that registration's email and password. An unknown email, a wrong password, and an already verified account all produce the same failure result, reveal nothing about whether an account exists, and change nothing. Only an unverified registration whose email and password both match is removed. Its username and email may then be registered again. If verification finishes first, the later cancellation fails and the account stays verified. If cancellation finishes first, verification finds no registration and creates no account. A verified account cannot be cancelled by this path. (DEC-049)

A taken username, an email held by an unverified registration, and an email held by a verified account all produce the same registration failure. That result does not say which value is taken or whether an account is verified, and no second account is created. (DEC-051)

A login with a verified email and the correct password opens that user's personal Calendar. An unknown email, an unverified registration, and a wrong password all produce the same failure result and do not reveal whether an account exists. (DEC-025)

### 4.2 Recover the password

Starting recovery shows the same result for an unknown email, an unverified registration, and a verified account. That result does not reveal whether an account exists or is verified. Starting recovery sends the one-time proof only when the email belongs to a verified account. An unknown email and an unverified registration send nothing. (DEC-051, DEC-075)

Completion submits the new password and that one-time proof. An unknown email, an unverified registration, a proof that does not match, a proof that is no longer valid, and a second use of the same proof all produce the same failure result. That result does not reveal whether an account exists, and it changes nothing. Sending a later proof makes every unused earlier proof no longer valid. The newest proof can still complete recovery. How long a proof remains valid stays open in DEC-023. Recovery does not verify an unverified registration. (DEC-066, DEC-076)

Completion sets a new password, and the previous password fails immediately. Every session already open for that account ends and cannot continue editing. A save that has been submitted but has not finished writes nothing, and the state before recovery completed remains. A save that finished before recovery completed is kept. Completion itself does not open the personal Calendar. A later login with the new password opens it. (DEC-061, DEC-073)

### 4.3 Keep a personal Calendar

The verified user has a personal Calendar. The user sees both public and private Events on it. No other user can open it in the first delivery. A logged-out person, including the owner after logout, cannot open the personal Calendar and cannot see any Event on it. Continuing requires login. After login, the owner sees that owner's Calendar. (DEC-014, DEC-016, DEC-038)

### 4.4 Save an Event

A new Event is private unless the creator chooses public before saving. A save requires a title, a start, and an end after the start. After outer whitespace is removed, the title must still contain a character. Location and description may be empty. There is no date-only Event. A save that lacks a required value does not write, and a stored Event remains unchanged. A successful edit of a non-repeating Event replaces its stored content immediately and does not keep the previous content. A successful deletion removes that Event immediately, does not restore it, and excludes it from later conflict comparison. A failed or cancelled deletion leaves the stored Event unchanged. (DEC-034)

The same account may save twice. Each successful save replaces the stored Event with that save's complete content. When both succeed, the stored Event is the content of the save that finishes last. A failed save writes nothing. A save that finishes after account deletion has completed writes nothing. If the save finishes first and account deletion then completes, deletion still removes the login, personal Calendar, and every Event, including that save. An operation that does not finish leaves the prior state. (DEC-039, DEC-050)

An Event that has already ended, an Event that crosses the current instant, and a series that starts in the past may be saved. (DEC-017, DEC-019)

### 4.5 Change one occurrence or the series

Deleting one occurrence removes only that occurrence and keeps it absent. The absence remembers that occurrence's original start instant. A later clock-time change on the same weekday does not create the new clock time on that date. Other occurrences on that weekday that have not started use the new time. A later clock-time change also leaves a deleted daily date and a deleted monthly date empty. Other dates that have not started use the new time. A weekday change does not move the absence onto the new weekday, and the new weekday's other occurrences appear. Timezone re-expansion does not recreate that occurrence at the new clock instant. (DEC-052, DEC-067, DEC-074)

A later series save may change occurrences that have not started, but it does not restore a deleted occurrence and does not reattach a detached occurrence. If the series save finishes first and the deletion or detachment finishes later, the later change wins. A detachment that finishes later stores the complete content submitted with that detachment. Changes the series save already made are not on that occurrence. Deleting the series removes those absence records with the series. The absence is not shown as a cancelled Event. (DEC-031, DEC-050, DEC-068)

Modifying one occurrence changes only that occurrence and detaches it from the series. Changing or deleting the series does not affect a detached occurrence. Deleting the series removes occurrences that are still attached and keeps detached occurrences. A series time change applies only to attached occurrences that have not started. An attached occurrence has occurred when its start has been reached, including while it is still in progress. A series time change and a timezone re-expansion do not move it. This applies to daily, weekly, and monthly series. (DEC-019, DEC-029)

The creator may later make one occurrence or the whole series public or private. Changing one occurrence's visibility detaches it. A series visibility change updates every occurrence still attached, including occurrences that have started, and does not change a detached occurrence. For an occurrence that has started, a series save that changes visibility and does not change title, location, or description updates visibility only and leaves its time and other content unchanged. A series change to title, location, or description updates every still-attached occurrence, including one that has started, and that started occurrence keeps its time. A detached occurrence keeps its own text, time, and visibility. In one series save, each field follows its own rule. A started occurrence that is still attached receives the new visibility, title, location, and description, and keeps its time. An occurrence that has not started receives the new time, visibility, title, location, and description. (DEC-017, DEC-032, DEC-033, DEC-054)

### 4.6 Repeat

Daily occurs on every calendar day in the user's current calendar timezone, at the start clock time, for the start-to-end duration. Weekly occurs only on the weekday of its start. Monthly occurs on the same date each month, and that date must exist in every month. None of the three ends automatically. The choice among non-repeating, daily, weekly, and monthly is fixed at creation. A later edit cannot change it. An edit that attempts the change writes nothing, and the stored Event remains unchanged. The creator may delete the Event and create another with a different kind. (DEC-035)

If a monthly date does not exist in every month, the preview names the types of month that lack the date and lists the concrete missing dates only inside the same 90 calendar dates used for conflict comparison, starting on the save date and including that date. It does not list every such month for the life of the series. The series is not saved. The product does not move the date to the month's last day and does not skip the month. (DEC-018, DEC-019, DEC-053, DEC-065)

### 4.7 Conflict warning

Saving compares the Event with every other Event that creator still owns. In the first delivery that set is the personal Calendar. The 90 days are 90 calendar dates in the creator's current calendar timezone, starting on the calendar date of the save and including that date. Other repeating Events are expanded across those dates. The occurrence or series being saved is not compared with its own attached occurrences. A detached occurrence is another Event the creator still owns. Saving the series compares it with that occurrence and warns when they overlap. One saved occurrence is compared only with other Events. (DEC-020, DEC-058)

Intervals overlap only when they share a positive length. An end that equals the next start is not an overlap. An overlap is warned when the shared time falls inside the window, including an overlap entirely before the save instant and an overlap with an occurrence that has already started. An occurrence that crosses the far boundary counts when any positive shared length is inside the window. A save with no overlap inside the window lists no overlapping Event and still succeeds. A series save still says the repetition continues after 90 days. That sentence is not a conflict warning. When overlaps exist inside the window, the warning lists each overlapping Event's title, start, and end, does not show an email address, and does not block saving. Saving a series also says the repetition continues after 90 days. That sentence means only that the series continues. It does not mean a conflict was found after 90 days. An overlap entirely after the window is not detected and is not listed. (DEC-030, DEC-036, DEC-053)

### 4.8 Timezone

Registration sets the calendar timezone from the browser. The user may change it. Entry, recurrence, the 90-day window, and displayed clock times use the current timezone. When the timezone changes and the series is not edited, an attached occurrence that has already occurred keeps its absolute instant and changes only its displayed clock time. An attached occurrence that has not yet occurred is expanded again at the same clock time in the new timezone. A future occurrence whose doubled start the creator has already chosen keeps that chosen absolute instant, and the new timezone changes only its displayed clock time. A detached or individually deleted occurrence is unchanged. A non-repeating Event keeps its absolute start and end, and only its displayed clock time changes. (DEC-018, DEC-027, DEC-070)

A local time that does not exist cannot be saved when the user is entering it. A local time that occurs twice must be chosen by the user when the user is entering it. A generated occurrence keeps its clock-face duration. If its generated start does not exist on that date, no occurrence is created, the series remains, and the creator can see that the date was skipped. If its generated start occurs twice, no occurrence is created until the creator chooses one instant in the timezone that applies at the choice. The creator can see that a choice is required. Before that choice, it is not included in conflict comparison. When the generated start exists and the generated end does not, or the interval would cross a missing local hour, that occurrence is skipped, the series remains, and the creator can see that the date was skipped. It is not shortened and is not stored with a nonexistent clock time. After a later timezone change, a skipped date stays skipped, and an unchosen doubled start stays unchosen. A later series time change also leaves that skipped date empty, even when the new clock time exists on that date. Other dates that have not started use the new time. The creator is still offered that start's two original absolute instants, labeled in the timezone that applies at the choice, even when the current timezone no longer doubles the clock time. (DEC-028, DEC-057, DEC-069, DEC-071)

### 4.9 Delete the account

The user may delete the account. The login, the personal Calendar, and every Event on it are deleted immediately and are not restored. Deletion has one result: those three disappear together, or the account stays unchanged. A deletion that stops early does not remove the login while leaving the Events, and does not remove the Events while leaving the login. The username is not assigned again. The email may register a new account with a new username. The new account does not own the deleted Events. What happens when the account still belongs to a team is unresolved and cannot occur in the first delivery. (DEC-022, DEC-039)

## 5. Later confirmed behavior

### 5.1 Search and the personal calendar page

Username search returns one user. Nickname search may return several users. A personal calendar link uses the username. Each search result shows the current nickname and the username. With no nickname, the display name is the username and the result still includes the username. The email is not shown. A username search matches the entire username. A nickname search lists only accounts whose current stored nickname equals the entire searched text. A search for `Sam` finds a nickname saved from ` Sam `. A search for ` Sam ` does not. It does not list a nickname that merely contains that text. Another person's username does not appear in a username search merely because it equals some nickname. A logged-out person cannot search. A logged-in search with no match returns no user and does not say whether the username never existed or was retired. An empty search returns no user and does not list accounts. (DEC-015, DEC-048, DEC-062, DEC-064)

After an account is deleted, username search finds no user. Nickname search does not list that deleted account. Opening its old personal-calendar link opens no Calendar and shows no Event, whether or not the visitor is logged in. A later account that reuses the email has its own username and its own link. The old username and the old link do not open that new account. (DEC-047)

A logged-in user who can open a Calendar sees a public Event's title, start, end, location, description, username, and current nickname. With no nickname, that user sees the username. An empty location or description is shown empty. The email is not shown. A private Event is not visible and does not reveal its existence. Another logged-in viewer sees the same absolute start and end converted to that viewer's current calendar timezone, and the timezone is shown. The creator continues to see the creator's timezone. The stored instant does not change. Whether two Events overlap remains an absolute comparison. Opening another person's public Calendar requires login. (DEC-016, DEC-017, DEC-037, DEC-056)

### 5.2 Sharing links

The user creates and revokes links to that user's personal Calendar. The person who created a team creates and revokes links to that team Calendar. One Calendar may have more than one link. Revoking one link removes only that address. The Calendar, its Events, and the other links remain. A logged-in user who can open that Calendar sees the same public Events through the link. Private Events stay hidden. A logged-out person sees no Events through a live link. A revoked personal link, a team link revoked while its creator remains a member, a team link that stops because its creator left, a team link after dissolution, and a sharing link after account deletion open no Calendar and show no Event to a logged-out or logged-in person. The result does not say whether the Calendar used to exist. (DEC-040, DEC-059, DEC-072)

### 5.3 Teams

A user may create more than one team and belong to more than one team. Each team has exactly one team Calendar. The person who creates a team becomes a member immediately and can create the first team Event. While that person remains a member, that person is the only administrator and handles invitations, removal, dissolution, and creating or revoking the team Calendar's sharing links. The creator invites by username, and the invited person joins only by accepting. (DEC-021, DEC-042)

A member creates Events on that Calendar and may modify or delete only Events that member created. The creator of an Event that remains after the member leaves may still modify or delete it. The creator cannot edit or see another person's private Event. Leaving or removal ends membership and leaves existing Events in place. An Event that remains after its creator leaves stays on the team Calendar until dissolution. An ended team Event stays there until dissolution and continues to participate in its creator's conflict comparison. Dissolution moves that ended Event with the other Events. The rule that an ended occurrence stays on the personal Calendar applies only to an Event stored on that personal Calendar. (DEC-021, DEC-046)

When the creator leaves, membership and administration both end and nobody succeeds. The team and its Calendar remain. Other members remain and may still create their own Events. Pending invitations end and can no longer be accepted. Existing team sharing links stop opening. The Calendar and its Events are not deleted for that reason. (DEC-042)

A current member can open the team Calendar, see every public Event, and see private Events that member created. That member cannot see another person's private Event. Any other logged-in user can open the team Calendar and sees only public Events. A logged-out person cannot open it and sees no Event. (DEC-043)

A former member opens a list in that member's own account containing only Events that member still owns on teams the member has left. From the list, the former member can see, modify, and delete those Events. The former member cannot open the rest of the team Calendar, cannot see another person's Events, and cannot create a team Event or manage the team. Current members still see the retained Event on the team Calendar according to its visibility. Removal uses this same list. When the former member later accepts an invitation and becomes a current member of that team again, the list entry for that team ends. Those Events remain only on the team Calendar, and the member edits them there. (DEC-041, DEC-055)

An unknown username creates no invitation and no account. An unverified username and a retired username do the same. An existing member receives no invitation, and that membership stays unchanged. A pending invitation to the same person for the same team is not duplicated, and the pending invitation remains. Only the invited account can accept or decline, and only while that account is logged in. Another logged-in user cannot accept it and does not become a member. Acceptance after the invitation has ended does not add a member. An invitation ends when it is declined, cancelled by the creator, ended because the creator leaves, or ended because the team is dissolved. A verified invited account sees that invitation in the account after logging in. The administrator sees current members and pending invitations. Each row shows the current nickname and the username, or the username when there is no nickname, and does not show the email. A current member sees the member list with those fields and does not see pending invitations. (DEC-044, DEC-060)

The creator may dissolve the team while that creator is the only current member, and while other members remain. The team Calendar, memberships, and pending invitations end. Each Event moves to its original creator's personal Calendar, including an Event left by a former member. An absence moves with its series. Visibility is unchanged. The original creator can still modify or delete Events that creator owns. This does not allow dissolution after the creator has already left. (DEC-045, DEC-052)

Creating a team and its one team Calendar, accepting an invitation, leaving, removal, and dissolution that moves every Event each finish entirely or leave the prior state unchanged. Dissolution does not leave some Events moved and others still on the team Calendar. An Event is not left in two Calendars, or in none, because a transition stopped halfway. (DEC-063)

Once teams exist, conflict comparison includes team Events the creator still owns. (DEC-020)

## 7. States

| Object | States | Transitions |
|---|---|---|
| Registration | Unverified; verified; cancelled | Registration sends a one-time proof to the registration email. A matching proof makes it verified. Cancellation removes an unverified registration. The last of verification or cancellation to finish determines the result. |
| Account | Verified; deleted | Deletion removes the login, personal Calendar, and its Events together, or leaves them all in place. |
| Session | Open; ended | Logout or completed recovery ends it. Recovery completion does not open a new session. |
| Event repetition | Non-repeating; daily; weekly; monthly | The kind is chosen at creation and does not change. |
| Occurrence | Attached and not started; attached and started; absent; detached; skipped; waiting for a doubled-start choice | A start instant moves it to started. Deletion makes it absent. Modifying one occurrence detaches it. A missing generated time skips it. An unchosen doubled start waits. |
| Team | Absent; active with its creator; active after the creator left; dissolved | Create makes it active. The creator leaving leaves it active with no administrator. Dissolution ends it and moves its Events. |
| Invitation | Absent; pending; ended | A verified username creates pending. Decline, cancellation, the creator leaving, or dissolution ends it. |
| Sharing link | Live; stopped | Revocation, the creator leaving, dissolution, or account deletion stops that address. Other live links remain. |
